# Final Project Summary

## Project Title

Conflict-Induced Displacement and Settlement Abandonment Detection Using Geospatial Analysis —
Borno State, Nigeria (2015–2023)

## Study Area

Borno State, northeastern Nigeria.

## Aim

To develop an uncertainty-aware geospatial framework for detecting settlement contraction and
evaluating possible conflict-induced abandonment between 2015 and 2023.

## Scope

The analysis screened **2,455 settlements across all 27 LGAs** of Borno State.

Detailed spectral and manual validation was conducted for **13 settlements across six LGAs**.

## Data Sources

- WorldPop population estimates for 2015 and 2023
- Landsat imagery
- settlement-location data
- Local Government Area boundaries
- built-up and spectral change indicators

## Methodology

1. Settlement database preparation and harmonisation.
2. Assignment of settlements to LGAs.
3. Integration of 2015 and 2023 WorldPop estimates.
4. Population and built-up change calculations.
5. Statistical abandonment screening.
6. Selection of 13 validation settlements.
7. Preparation of Landsat image pairs.
8. Comparison of RGB, NDVI and NDBI evidence.
9. Structured manual evidence adjudication.
10. Final ranking, classification, mapping and reporting.

## Final Results

- Probable abandonment: **5**
- Possible abandonment: **1**
- Uncertain: **4**
- Not supported: **2**
- Reconstruction or reoccupation: **1**
- Confirmed abandonment: **0**

The highest-ranked settlement was **Wumbi in Kala Balge LGA**.

No settlement was classified as confirmed abandonment because independent field or
very-high-resolution corroboration was unavailable.

## Main Interpretation

The results demonstrate that settlement abandonment cannot be determined reliably from one
population, built-up or spectral indicator. A defensible conclusion requires the triangulation
of demographic, physical, spectral and manually interpreted evidence.

## Planning Relevance

The outputs can support:

- humanitarian settlement monitoring;
- field-verification prioritisation;
- displacement and return assessment;
- reconstruction monitoring;
- post-conflict spatial planning;
- future Web GIS development.
