# Project Report: Settlement Change in Conflict-Affected Borno State

## Background

Settlement decline in a conflict-affected region is difficult to describe from one dataset alone. A fall in night-time lights may reflect power disruption. A reduction in the built signal may not mean that every building is empty. Population models can also disagree with what satellite imagery appears to show.

I built this project around that uncertainty rather than trying to remove it from the story.

## What I did

I screened **2,455 settlements across all 27 LGAs in Borno State** and brought together four main evidence groups: built-signal change, VIIRS night-time lights, conflict exposure and modelled population change.

From the statewide screening, I retained **13 settlements** for closer review. The final interpretation was based on the way the indicators agreed or disagreed, not on one variable alone.

## What I found

Three settlements showed the strongest combined evidence of contraction:

1. **Mitile** in Abadam;
2. **Johana** in Kala Balge; and
3. **Wumbi** in Kala Balge.

Seven other settlements showed important change signals, but population evidence did not support a simple abandonment interpretation. Three more retained change signals but had insufficient population evidence for a stronger demographic conclusion.

The final public interpretation is therefore:

- **3 priority contraction cases**;
- **7 priority change-signal cases**;
- **3 retained change-signal cases**; and
- **0 confirmed abandoned settlements**.

## Why I stopped short of calling places abandoned

Across the 13 detailed cases, only three show modelled population decline. Four show population increase, three are relatively stable and three have insufficient population evidence.

That matters because physical decline and demographic decline are not always the same thing.

Calling a settlement abandoned from a single satellite signal would be a stronger claim than the evidence supports. I therefore use the maps to identify places that deserve closer investigation rather than to replace field verification.

## What the result means

The project can help narrow a very large study area to locations where several indicators point to unusual or potentially important change.

That makes the result useful for follow-up work such as high-resolution imagery review, field investigation, comparison with displacement records and local knowledge, or repeated monitoring of settlement recovery and decline.

## Important limitations

WorldPop is a modelled population surface rather than a settlement census. Night-time lights depend on electricity availability and settlement size. Built-signal change does not tell us whether a structure is occupied. Conflict exposure also does not prove that one recorded event caused a particular spatial change.

The 13 detailed cases are not statistically representative of every settlement in Borno State.

## What I would add next

The strongest next step is independent local evidence. Field verification, better displacement records and very-high-resolution imagery could help distinguish damaged, temporarily vacated, recovered and permanently depopulated settlements.

A longer time series could also show whether the current contraction signals are temporary or persistent.
