PROJECT TITLE
Conflict-Induced Displacement and Settlement Abandonment Detection Using Geospatial Analysis — Borno State, Nigeria (2015–2023)

PROJECT SUMMARY
This project developed a multi-stage geospatial framework for detecting settlement contraction and potential abandonment in conflict-affected Borno State. The analysis screened 2,455 settlements across 27 LGAs and integrated WorldPop population estimates, built-up-area change, Landsat RGB imagery, NDVI, NDBI and structured manual adjudication.

PROBLEM STATEMENT
Conventional field monitoring of conflict-affected settlements in Borno State is constrained by insecurity, restricted access, incomplete records and the large geographic extent of the affected area. A transparent geospatial method was required to identify settlements showing persistent physical contraction and distinguish probable abandonment from stability, uncertainty and reconstruction.

METHODOLOGY
1. Harmonised 2,455 settlement locations across all 27 Borno LGAs.
2. Integrated WorldPop population estimates for 2015 and 2023.
3. Calculated population and built-up-area change indicators.
4. Developed a four-indicator settlement-change typology.
5. Selected 13 candidate settlements for detailed validation.
6. Prepared 2015 and 2023 Landsat image pairs.
7. Compared RGB, NDVI and NDBI evidence.
8. Conducted structured manual adjudication.
9. Produced final classifications, evidence rankings, maps and publication outputs.

KEY RESULTS
- Settlements screened: 2,455
- LGAs covered: 27
- Detailed validation cases: 13
- Validation LGAs: 6
- Probable abandonment: 5
- Possible abandonment: 1
- Uncertain: 4
- Not supported: 2
- Reconstruction or reoccupation: 1
- Confirmed abandonment: 0
- Highest-ranked settlement: Wumbi, Kala Balge LGA
- Population estimate increased from 5,961,054 in 2015 to 7,342,382 in 2023.
- Mitile was statistically supported but not corroborated by Landsat spectral evidence.
- Mairamri showed reconstruction or reoccupation rather than abandonment.

TOOLS AND SKILLS
Python, GeoPandas, Pandas, Rasterio, Landsat remote sensing, WorldPop, NDVI, NDBI, settlement-level change detection, spatial joins, cartography, evidence integration, uncertainty assessment, technical reporting and portfolio communication.

CONCLUSION
The project demonstrated that settlement abandonment should not be inferred from a single indicator. Combining demographic, built-up, spectral and manual evidence produced a more cautious and defensible classification framework for humanitarian assessment and spatial planning.
